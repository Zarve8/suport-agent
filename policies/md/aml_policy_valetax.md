c) Money Laundering, Terrorism Financing and Proliferation Financing
Money laundering (ML) refers to the process by which the proceeds of illegal activities are transformed into
apparently legitimate money or other assets. The aim is to obscure the illicit origins of these proceeds so they
can be used without detection of the initial criminal activity. Money laundering is commonly associated with
crimes such as drug trafficking, terrorism, corruption, tax evasion, and financial fraud.
Terrorism Financing (TF) refers to the provision of funds or providing financial support to individual terrorists
or non-state actors, most often with the intention to carry out acts of terror. The funds may be used to
purchase weapons, train operatives, pay operatives, and conduct operations, among other activities. It is a
major concern for international security agencies because the financing mechanisms often exploit legitimate
channels and can be difficult to detect.
Proliferation Financing (PF) refers to the act of providing funds or financial services which are used, in whole or
in part, for the manufacture, acquisition, possession, development, export, trans-shipment, brokering,
transport, transfer, stockpiling, or use of nuclear, chemical or biological weapons and their means of delivery
and related materials (including both technologies and dual-use goods used for non- legitimate purposes), in
contravention of national laws or, where applicable, international obligations.
5. Independent Compliance audit
The FIAML Regulations 2018 requires the audit process to be carried out independently.
The audit functions should be independent of, and separate to the executive team dealing with the company’s
Anti- Money Laundering (‘AML’), Combating Financing of Terrorism (‘CFT’) and Counter-Proliferation Financing
(‘CPF’) processes.

The auditor must not have been involved in the development of the risk assessment, or the establishment,
implementation, or maintenance of the company’s AML / CFT / CPF programme.
An independent audit function shall be appointed by the company, in order to test the ML and TF policies,
procedures and controls that should be maintained.

AML / CFT / CPF risk assessment, policies, or procedures.

1.1. Procedures for Verification of Residential Address Using Proof of Identification
The Company may, under a risk-based approach, accept certain government-issued proof of identification
containing the holder's full residential address as evidence of residential address in lieu of a separate Proof of

At present, this alternative verification method is restricted exclusively to citizens of Malaysia presenting a valid

The Company reserves the right to require a separate Proof of Address document at any stage of the customer
relationship, where considered necessary, to satisfy Customer Due Diligence obligations.

1.1.1. Eligibility Criteria
A Malaysian National Identity Card may be accepted as evidence of residential address only where all of the
following conditions are met:
• the document is genuine and passes all document authenticity verification procedures;
• the document is valid and unexpired;
• the document clearly displays the holder's full residential address;
• the residential address is complete and legible;
• the identity of the applicant has been satisfactorily verified;
• no inconsistencies or discrepancies have been identified during the onboarding process;
• the customer has been assessed as Low or Medium Risk;
• no Enhanced Due Diligence measures are required.

Annex 5: Red Flag Indicators
Red flags are indicators of potential ML/TF/PF risks. The presence of one or more red flags does not automatically indicate suspicious activity but
should trigger enhanced scrutiny and possible escalation.

The below list is not exhaustive and its content is purely provided to reflect examples of possible red flags.
1. The deposit or withdrawal of unusually large amounts of cash from an account;
2. Unwillingness to provide CDD documentation on beneficial owners/ controllers;
3. Deposits or withdrawals at a frequency that is inconsistent with the financial institution’s understanding of that customer and their circumstances;
4. Transactions involving the unexplained movement of funds, either as cash or wire transfers;
5. Payments received from, or requests to make payments to, unknown or un-associated third parties;
6. Personal and business-related money flows that are difficult to distinguish from each other;
7. Financial activity which is inconsistent with the legitimate or expected activity of the Client;
8. An account or business relationship becomes active after a period of dormancy;
9. The customer is unable or reluctant to provide details or credible explanations for establishing a business relationship, opening an account or
conducting a transaction;
10. The customer holds multiple accounts for no apparent commercial or other reason;
11. Bank drafts cashed in for foreign currency;
12. Early surrender of an insurance policy incurring substantial loss;
13. Frequent early repayment of loans;
14. Frequent transfers indicated as loans sent from relatives;
15. Funds transferred to a charity or NPO with suspected links to a terrorist organization;
16. High level of funds placed on store value cards;
17. Insurance policy being closed with a request for the payment to be made to a third party;
18. Large amounts of cash from unexplained sources;
19. Obtained loan and repaid balance in cash;
